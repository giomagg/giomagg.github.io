Research

My research is devoted to understanding the complex interdependencies between society and technology. Influenced by the philosophy of Foucault, Latour, and Deleuze and Guattari, I work on modelling (linguistic) interactions between human and non-human entities in the (online) social and public space. So far, my work contributed to the study of political communication, journalism and alternative media, radical-right extremism, disinformation, and the discursive polarization process.

My approach is rooted in methodologies from the computational social sciences, collecting digital trace data using web scraping and crawling techniques, and leveraging network analysis and natural language processing to investigate these phenomena as complex systems.

In the highlights below, I summarize some of the research projects devoted to studying the online public sphere that I conducted over the past years.

I also enjoy producing interactive visualisations of academic literature on different topics. The goal is to understand the complex semantic relations in how we study topics related to politics and communication, visualising both the structure of an academic literature, its main influences, along with gaps in research. Highlights
Weizenbaum project – 2025
A bibliometric analysis of the literature on disinformation and its funders

[In Progress]
In this project in partnership with the Weizenbaum Institut, I map academic citations, co-authorship relations, and funding streams in of the literature on disinformation using traditional and LLM-based natural language processing. Preliminary results confirm that, in studying the societal effects of social media (especially in the dominating fields of communication, psychology, and political science), the field is highly influenced by the US academic tradition, and the study of its media and political system. On the other hand, European scholars are highly referenced in the study of data governance – often funded by public national and European public authorities. On average, scholars from the global majority world remain peripheric in the field. These trends seem to be reinforced by co-authorship and funding patterns.


OID Project – 2025
Information and Democracy Semantic Landscape

Access project. Access code.
This work is an NLP-based interactive mapping of the literature on information ecosystems and democracy as defined in Mansell et al. (2025). In it, I leveraged word embeddings, umap dimensionality reduction, and hdbscan clustering to derive a semantic landscape in which each point represents one (or more) paragraph(s) of an academic source. I then employed a hybrid approach combining qualitative content analysis and topic modelling (BERTopic) to label the clusters. This project benefited from the advice of Prof. David Chavalarias and Dr. Paul Bauchaud at the CNRS Institute for Complex Systems, as well as of the OID scientific director Prof. Robin Mansell (LSE). The project is part of my work for the Observatory on Information and Democracy.



Master Thesis - 2024
Structuring the Public Debate.
Party-Press Alignment and Discursive Polarization in the French Immigration and Climate Change Debate

Full Manuscript

In my thesis I explore whether the arguments developed by Benkler, Farris, and Roberts in their 2018 book "Network Propaganda" apply in contexts outside of the US – i.e., in countries with different media and political systems. In dialogue with the literature in comparative media studies, political communication, and political science, I leverage computational techniques to estimate the discursive polarization levels in France between 2017 and 2019 and its relation to party-press alignment on issue salience. My analysis finds little evidence of polarization in the French media system and scarce proof of a correlation between the two variables.

Supervisors: Prof. Caterina Froio (Sciences Po, CEE); Prof. Pedro Ramaciotti Morales (CNRS, Institute for Complex Systems)


Master Paper - 2023
Exploring the #NordStream Discourse on Twitter

Full Paper
In this work, together with Luis Denart, we explored the Twitter discussion around the explosion of the Nord Stream 2 pipeline in 2022 in the context of the Russian invasion of Ukraine. Using social network analysis and large language models we find clearly identifiable communities showing different discourses and narratives concerning the event. The project was produced for a CSS class taught by Prof. Jean-Philippe Cointet, Prof. Marton Karsai, and Armin Pournaki.

Master Paper - 2023
Humans, Threat, or Management?
Framing Immigration in the Media during the 2022 Italian Election

Full paper
This was a research paper produced for the class "From the Margins to the Mainstream – Far-Right Politics in Europe and Beyond" by prof. Caterina Froio. In it, I mapped the media discussion on immigration in Italy during the 2022 election using web crawling methods and social network analysis, and qualitatively analyse the frames used by different communities to talk about immigration.

Master Paper - 2022
Evaluating Fox News' impact on US senators' political communication

Project Summary
In this project, together with Christoph Mautner Markhof, we investigated the influence of Fox News on the Twitter communication of US senators using sentiment analysis.


Bachelor's Paper - 2019
Mapping the Connection between Celebrities and Politicians on Twitter

This is my first ever computational social science project, conducted under the supervision of prof. Chris Bail during a course at the Venice International Universtity. Together with the class we mapped the connections between US celebrities and politicians from the US house of representatives on Twitter using social network analysis. The results show little connection – in terms of retweets, citations, and likes – between the two classes (in the vizualization below, politicians are on the left and celebrities on the right).
